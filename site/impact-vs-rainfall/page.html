This is scoping work, not an anticipatory-action trigger.

What the data show

  1. Flood impact follows the rainy season. {{PCT_JASO}}% of people affected were reported in alerts from July to October, the four wettest months.
  2. 2022 stands out, with {{WORST_PEOPLE}} people affected ({{WORST_PCT}}% of the five-year total). {{TOP2_TEXT}}
  3. The year-to-year link with rainfall does not hold up. With ERA5, annual rainfall and people affected correlate at r = {{R_ERA5}}, but that rests on five years and is driven by 2022. It falls to {{R_ERA5_DETR}} once ERA5's trend is removed, and the other products give r = {{R_CHIRPS}} (CHIRPS) and {{R_IMERG}} (IMERG). {{WETTEST_TEXT}}
  4. Month to month, the link is mostly the shared season. Monthly rainfall and people affected rank-correlate (ρ ≈ {{RHO_TOTAL}}), but once each month's usual rainfall or its trend is removed, wetter-than-usual months are not generally worse (ρ {{RHO_ANOM_RANGE}}). One month carries most of the linear correlation: {{BIG_MONTH}} ({{BIG_PEOPLE}} people affected) was much wetter than usual in all three products. Pearson r is {{R_ANOM_RANGE}} with it and {{R_ANOM_RANGE_X}} without it.
  5. ERA5's drying over CAR is very likely an artefact. ERA5 has national rainfall falling by {{TREND_ERA5_ABS}} mm per decade since 1998, which makes 2021–2025 its five driest years. CHIRPS, which blends in rain-gauge data, shows no significant change ({{TREND_CHIRPS}} ± {{TREND_CHIRPS_CI}} mm per decade) and IMERG shows {{TREND_IMERG}}.
  6. The products agree on the season, not on anomalies. Their monthly totals correlate at r ≥ {{R_PROD_TOTAL_MIN}}, but their monthly anomalies only at {{R_PROD_ANOM_RANGE}} and their detrended annual totals at {{R_PROD_ANNUAL_RANGE}}. Which months and years count as "wet" over CAR depends heavily on the product, a problem for any national-rainfall trigger.

When floods are reported

Each row of the compilation is an alert: a flood reported in a commune by OCHA sub-offices, RRM partners, IOM, government services and others, with the people and households affected. Alerts are dated when they were raised, which may be some days after the flooding.

The rainfall record

National mean rainfall from three independent products: ERA5 (ECMWF reanalysis, monthly, from 1981) and IMERG (NASA GPM satellite estimate, late run v7, daily, from 1998), both from the team's raster-stats database, and CHIRPS v3 (UCSB Climate Hazards Center, satellite blended with rain-gauge data, monthly, from 1981), read directly from the CHC's cloud-optimised files. All three have the same seasonal cycle, but ERA5 drifts: in 1981–1990 it averaged {{ERA5_80S}} mm a year against {{CHIRPS_80S}} for CHIRPS, and in 2016–2025 {{ERA5_LAST10}} mm against {{CHIRPS_LAST10}}.

ERA5's trend over CAR is very likely an artefact. Over 1998–2025 ERA5 shows {{TREND_ERA5}} ± {{TREND_ERA5_CI}} mm per decade, and over its full 1981–2025 record {{TREND_ERA5_FULL}} mm per decade (about {{TREND_ERA5_FULL_PCT}}% of its 2001–2020 average per decade). Over the same periods CHIRPS shows {{TREND_CHIRPS}} ± {{TREND_CHIRPS_CI}} and {{TREND_CHIRPS_FULL}} ± {{TREND_CHIRPS_FULL_CI}} mm per decade, neither significant, and IMERG shows {{TREND_IMERG}} ± {{TREND_IMERG_CI}}. A drift in the reanalysis, which assimilates very few rain-gauge observations over CAR, is the likely explanation. Because of it, {{ERA5_RANK_TEXT}}, while CHIRPS places them across its range. The impact comparisons below therefore also use detrended rainfall.

Does more rain mean more people affected?

Two time scales: year by year (are wetter years worse?) and month by month (are wetter months worse?). National rainfall is a coarse stand-in for flood hazard. Many alerts are urban (Bangui) or riverine (Oubangui, Ouham), driven by local downpours or upstream river levels that a national mean cannot see.

Detrended rainfall is each product's rainfall minus its own linear trend, fitted per calendar month over 1998–2025 (the years all three products cover). It removes ERA5's drift, so a year counts as wet relative to its neighbours, not relative to the 1980s.

Method & caveats

  • Impact data. OCHA CAR's “Données inondations” compilation (sheet DATA FOR PBI): {{N_EVENTS}} alerts dated {{FIRST_ALERT}} to {{LAST_ALERT}}. A public version through 2024 ({{N_HDX}} alerts) is on HDX (CC BY). The copy used here adds {{N_EXTRA_2024}} later 2024 alerts and {{N_2025}} from 2025 that are not on HDX (and revises a few earlier rows slightly), so this page is password-protected. Coverage is whatever was reported and may vary by year and area. A month with no alert means nothing was recorded, not that nothing happened.
  • People affected is the Individus affecté column ({{N_NULL_PEOPLE}} alerts have no figure and count as zero), and households is Ménage affecté. Counts are summed across alerts, so a commune hit twice in a year counts twice.
  • Matching to communes. Commune names were normalised and matched to the FieldMaps COD adm3 names. {{N_NAME}} matched directly, and {{N_NAME_FIX}} needed a spelling fix (e.g. “4e Arrondissement” → “Arrondissement 4”, “Bria” → Samba-Boungou). {{N_OVERRIDE}} were reassigned by hand from the sous-préfecture, locality and coordinates (e.g. a row filed under the commune “Bangui” with locality “Mogoumba” → Mongoumba). Four of those overrides are new since the notebook: checking each match against the compilation's own prefecture column found communes filed as “Mbaïki” or “Yéngou” whose locality is in Batangafo, Bégoua, Zinga or Bria. Each row's method is in match_method in the processed events file. Every alert matched.
  • Prefectures follow the COD boundaries: 16 prefectures plus Bangui. Alerts the compilation files under prefectures created in the 2020–21 reform (Ouham-Fafa, Lim-Pendé, Mambéré) count under the COD prefecture that contains them (Ouham, Ouham-Pendé, Mambéré-Kadéï). Bimbo is in Ombella-M'Poko, not Bangui.
  • Rainfall. National (adm0) means. ERA5 stores monthly mean daily rates, converted to monthly totals. IMERG daily values are averaged per month and scaled to the month's length; months with fewer than 25 days of data are dropped. CHIRPS v3 monthly totals are averaged over the 0.05° pixels whose centres fall inside CAR, read window-only from the CHC's global cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs. Anomalies are against each product's own 2001–2020 monthly average. Detrended values are residuals from a least-squares line fitted per product and calendar month over 1998–2025; because the fits share the same years, detrended annual and cumulative totals are sums of the monthly residuals. Annual totals use complete years only (2026 is excluded).
  • Statistics. Trends are least-squares per calendar month, with a t-based 95% confidence interval (no autocorrelation adjustment). Correlations are Pearson r with two-sided p, plus Spearman ρ, over n = 5 years or n = 60 months. This is exploratory: many comparisons, no correction for multiple testing.
  • Not a trigger. No forecast is evaluated here. The repository README states the work is not currently for an anticipatory-action framework.
  • Source. The analysis started in exploration/ocha_impact.ipynb. This page is built by scripts/build_data.py and scripts/build_site.py in OCHA-DAP/ds-aa-caf-flooding. IMERG, CHIRPS and the detrending are not in the notebook. They were added as robustness checks.
